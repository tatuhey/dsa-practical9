SORTING RUNTIMES

From the values acquired via the SortsHarnessTest.java, we can see that insertion sort is the fastest in comparison to bubble sort and selection sort. Especially when the array is already ascending or nearly sorted. That is due to the fact that insertion sort swaps only when it is needed, rather than on every comparison. I cannot see any benefit of the nearly sorted to the bubble sort as mentioned in the lecture slide. On average, it apperas that selection sort is the best one out of the other two sorting algorithm. However, bubble sort works at the same rate as insertion sort for the ascending data type.


--

With regards to the implementation of merge sort and quick sort with varying pivot selection, we can observe that quick sort with median of three pivot selection results in the best average time out of the others. Followed by merge sort, then quick sort random. Quick sort with left most pivot selection is the slowest out of them all. That is due to pivot selection, which resulted in barely any good spit during the partition of the quick sort process. Quick sort with median of three pivot is the fastest on average, as it used the best pivot available, which is the middle of the array.
